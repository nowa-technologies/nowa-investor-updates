■  01

EVT complete, and key DVT checks cleared early.

Hardware typically moves through three validation stages before mass production:

EVT  ·  Engineering Validation Test

First build with real parts. Does the design work?

DVT  ·  Design Validation Test

Production-intent parts and tooling. Does the product meet the final specification and certification requirements?

PVT  ·  Production Validation Test

Pilot production run. Can the factory build it consistently at volume?

Reliability testing and certification are normally DVT work. We completed both during EVT, giving us a head start going into DVT and reducing technical risk before the next build.

12 / 12

reliability tests passed.

28 units were tested from 6 to 27 August. All 12 planned reliability tests passed:

✓  Heat and humidity  ·  5°C to 45°C over 48 hours

✓  Thermal shock  ·  −20°C to +70°C, 12 hours at each extreme

✓  Running hot and cold  ·  Powered on for 24 hours at each temperature extreme

✓  Charging temperature  ·  Required to stay below 45°C; the units stayed below 30°C

✓  Buttons, wheel, and ports  ·  100,000 button presses, 100,000 wheel turns, and 2,000 plug-ins

✓  Device drop test  ·  One-meter drop while fitted in its case

✓  Packaged drop test  ·  The retail box tested for courier-style drops

✓  Screen pull test  ·  500 g of pulling force for 8 hours

✓  Burn-in  ·  12 hours at full volume

✓  Salt spray  ·  24 hours in a salt-fog chamber

✓  Sunscreen and cleaners  ·  12 hours of exposure

✓  Print adhesion  ·  50 alcohol rubs and 300 tape cycles

■  An extreme stress test revealed one issue – and fixed

All 12 planned tests passed. In a separate, tougher-than-spec tumble test, a bare device was dropped from one meter into a rotating drum and rolled 100 times. The memory card came loose inside; the rest of the device held up. The fix is simple: the card is now glued to the board during assembly.

■  02

The manufacturing line ran smoothly.

Our factory set up a dedicated line for the 100-unit EVT run. Assembly was smooth from the first unit, with no line-level assembly issues. The factory team told us that is unusual for a first build, when fit or assembly problems often show up immediately.

The current plan is to use the same line for our first 3,000-unit production order.
