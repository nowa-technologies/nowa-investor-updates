■  01

EVT is complete, with key DVT work already behind us.

Hardware usually goes through three stages before mass production:

EVT  ·  Engineering Validation Test

Does the design work using real components?

DVT  ·  Design Validation Test

Does the final design, tooling, and production hardware meet our specifications?

PVT  ·  Production Validation Test

Can the factory build the product consistently at volume?

Reliability testing and certification usually happen during DVT. We completed both during EVT, giving us a head start on the next build and removing a meaningful layer of technical risk.

12 / 12

reliability tests passed.

Between 6 and 27 August, 28 devices went through our full reliability test plan:

✓  Heat and humidity  ·  5°C to 45°C over 48 hours

✓  Thermal shock  ·  −20°C to +70°C, with 12 hours at each extreme

✓  Operation in extreme temperatures  ·  Powered on for 24 hours at each extreme

✓  Charging temperature  ·  Required to stay below 45°C; our units stayed below 30°C

✓  Buttons, wheel, and ports  ·  100,000 button presses, 100,000 wheel turns, and 2,000 plug-ins

✓  Device drop  ·  One-metre drop while fitted in its case

✓  Packaged drop  ·  Retail packaging tested for courier-style drops

✓  Screen retention  ·  500g of pulling force for eight hours

✓  Burn-in  ·  12 hours at full volume

✓  Salt spray  ·  24 hours in a salt-fog chamber

✓  Sunscreen and cleaners  ·  12 hours of exposure

✓  Print adhesion  ·  50 alcohol rubs and 300 tape cycles

All 12 passed.

■  One tougher test found an issue, and helped us fix it

We also ran a separate, tougher-than-spec tumble test: an uncased device was dropped from one metre into a rotating drum and rolled 100 times. The memory card came loose, but the rest of the device held up. The fix was straightforward: the card will now be secured to the board during assembly.

■  02

The production line ran smoothly.

Our factory set up a dedicated line for the 100-unit EVT build. Assembly ran smoothly from the first unit, with no line-level issues. The factory team told us this is unusual for a first build, when fit and assembly problems often appear immediately.

We plan to use the same line for our first 3,000-unit production run.
