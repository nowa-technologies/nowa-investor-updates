■  01 · Why this month matters

We passed DVT’s tests one build early.

Three validation builds stand between a prototype and a product:

EVT  ·  Engineering Validation Test  ·  done

The first build with real parts. Does the design actually work?

DVT  ·  Design Validation Test  ·  next

Production-intent parts and tooling. Does it meet every spec, and pass certification?

PVT  ·  Production Validation Test

A pilot run on the real line. Can the factory build it at volume, the same way every time?

Reliability testing and certification belong to DVT. We passed both at EVT, on the first build. Getting there normally costs a whole extra round of parts and several weeks.

■  The one problem

One part came loose, in a test tougher than the spec.

They dropped a bare device from a meter into a rotating drum and rolled it a hundred times. Everything held except the memory card, which shook loose inside. It now gets glued to the board during assembly.

■  02 · The line

■  04 · What’s next

200 units, built like the real thing.

DVT is not a prototype batch. Real line, real workers, real quality checks, final colors, final spec. What comes off that line is what we sell.

About 100 of them go out for feedback to our research advisors, to creators and to families. The rest stay with the manufacturing team for its own internal testing.

Alongside that: FCC certification is already passed, materials for the 3,000-unit run are on order, and the production firmware is finished this month so the chip supplier can write it before the boards are assembled.
